# bakery-api

The Rust Worker behind `api.bakery.ihaveasegway.com`: the router, every Durable Object class, the
Queue consumer and the cron handler ([architecture.md](../../docs/architecture.md)). Since the
launch on 2026-11-06 it runs at api.bakery.ihaveasegway.com and locally on `wrangler dev`.

The number 19 in the route table's `pr` is the UI audit of 2026-11-08, which has no pull request
number of its own. The number 20 below, in the line the tests read and in the table's `pr`, is the
remaining-work day of 2026-11-09, which has none either; the tax rate's route was added on it. The
flight recorder's two routes carry 21 in the table's `pr`, also added on 2026-11-09; the line the
tests read stays at 20, since a row may be listed and served before it moves (as of 2026-11-09: 76
routes, 60 of them accepting a token, in `apps/web/public/docs/api/openapi.json`).

## Using a token

A signed-in sandbox has an API token (Q42 · Q-api-access; [security.md](../../docs/security.md#the-api-token)).
The console issues it with `POST /v1/sandboxes/{id}/token` and shows it once; `GET` shows its
version, times and masked form. It is signed like the session cookie, with `SESSION_HMAC_KEY` under
its own purpose, lives 24 h and dies with the sandbox. With it, `curl` calls every route whose Token
column says yes, with no cookie and no `Origin`:

```sh
curl 'http://127.0.0.1:8788/v1/sandboxes/<sandbox id>/menu' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <your token>'
curl -X POST 'http://127.0.0.1:8788/v1/sandboxes/<sandbox id>/orders' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <your token>' \
  -H 'Idempotency-Key: <a new ULID>' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"kind":"pickup","slot":"<a slot start>","lines":[{"variant":"<a variant>","qty":1}]}'
```

The router takes the token before the cookie, in this order, each step free until the last:

1. On a route that needs a session, a token in the query string (a parameter named `token`,
   `access_token`, `api_token`, `bearer` or `authorization`, or any value shaped like a signed
   token) is 400 `token` (`query`), and a token beside the session cookie is 400 `token` (`both`).
   A route whose Token column says no (the session, sign-out, the kitchen socket, issuing and
   rotating) answers 401 `token` (`cookie-only`): a token never mints its successor. A public
   route ignores `Authorization`.
2. The MAC under either key in constant time, the purpose, the payload's version and the expiry:
   401 `token` naming `token`, `purpose` (the session cookie's value sent as a token) or `expired`.
3. The token's sandbox against the path's `{id}`: another is the 404 of an id naming nothing.
4. `TOKEN_LIMIT`, keyed by the token's `tid`: 60 requests a minute, every request counted, reads
   too (the session's command rate, `COMMAND_LIMIT`); then 429 `rate-limit` with `Retry-After: 60`.
   Its own binding, so a token's spend never slows the console's session.
5. One read of the `Sandbox` (`GET /_token`): gone is 404, wiping is 410 `wiped`, and a token
   naming an older `token_v` is 401 `token` (`rotated`): "Rotate" stops the old token at once.

The request then carries the token's account, how it signed in and its sandbox, as a session
would, and every route behaves as for the cookie, sign-out's revocation aside: sign-out ends a
session, while a token ends with a rotation, its 24 h or the wipe. A token's answer has no CORS
headers and sets no cookie (a wipe by token re-issues none). The `Sandbox` keeps the last token's
`tid`, times and masked form (its first 6 and last 4 characters, the first 6 always `eyJ2Ij`),
never the token; issuing records the claims under the request's `Idempotency-Key`, so a retry
signs the same token again.

## What a sign-in stores

Google's `sub` and GitHub's numeric id become `HMAC-SHA256(ACCOUNT_HMAC_KEY, "google:" + sub)` or
`"github:" + id` in the Worker and are dropped there: the `Account` object, named `account:<that hex>`,
holds the hex, the sign-in kind, the day it was created, the last sign-in, the current sandbox and
its wipe time, today's sandbox count, revoked session ids for 24 h, and one timer that deletes it
30 days after the last sign-in. A demo account also holds its `baker-xxxx` username, the argon2id
PHC string and its recent failed sign-in times; the password never leaves the Worker. Nothing
else a provider sends (email, name, picture, login, tokens) is read past its parser.

## Events, the Queue and the children

Since 2026-11-09 a sandbox has 37 children: the flight recorder's `Recorder` (`recorder:<sandbox>`)
is seeded after the `Inbox`, before every child that writes events (see "The trace socket" below).
A new sandbox answers `seeding` until its first alarm has created its children in six
batches (PR 9's count: Crm and the Inbox since PR 8, today's `Dispatch` since PR 9, both listed
below): the `Catalog` (`catalog:<sandbox>`), the `Kitchen` (`kitchen:<sandbox>`), `Billing`
(`billing:<sandbox>`, with the seed's history as daily totals), the seed's 7 open `Schedule` days
(`schedule:<sandbox>:<YYYY-MM-DD>`: the menu's caps, then each open order's place held until its
Order's `held_until`, 15 business minutes after its placement or the birth, and confirmed when
the order is paid), the seed's 12 open orders (`order:<sandbox>:<ULID>`), its 8 catering events
(`catering:<sandbox>:<ULID>`, each replaying the seed's commands through the aggregate, its
history kept in the log with no outbox row; the accepted one is accepted at birth, so its deposit
hold is live for 15 business minutes) and the 3 days that hold the accepted and confirmed events'
places, each registered before its first write. Then it is `live` and appends `SandboxSeeded`. Later days are born on
first touch. The wipe calls `/_wipe` on every registered child by class.

Since PR 8 (2026-11-05) the count is 35, not 33, in five batches of 7: `Crm` (`crm:<sandbox>`) and the
`Inbox` (`inbox:<sandbox>`) join the first group, seeded before the orders so that a seeded paid
order's events find them born. `Customer` objects (`customer:<sandbox>:<persona>`) are made on
first touch and some are touched while seeding, so `children` ends above 35; the smoke scripts
assert at least 35. Alongside the routes above the consumer now also delivers to Crm, the Inbox
and the order's `Customer` (see the CRM section below), and `bakery-events` carries no note or
brief to the first two. New problems: `campaign` (422 `schedule-too-soon` with the earliest time,
or 409 naming the rule, such as `out-of-order`), `rebuild` (409, one already running) and `filter`
(400, an inbox filter the Worker does not know).

Every object that writes events (Sandbox, Catalog, Order, Billing, Schedule, CateringEvent) commits them with their outbox rows and
the command's key in one transaction, sends the rows to `bakery-events` as JSON strings under
`wait_until`, and keeps an `outbox` timer 30 s out for whatever the send left. The consumer in
`src/queue.rs` delivers each envelope to the subscribers `bakery_app::routing` names (the Kitchen,
Billing, since PR 5 the Order a payment's or refund's `ref` names, and since PR 6 the `Schedule`:
an Order event goes to the day its slot names, read from the Order, and `MenuPublished` to every
registered day from business today on, read from the Sandbox; since PR 7 the `CateringEvent` a
deposit's, balance's or invoice's `ref` names, and the `Schedule` day a catering event's date
names) and acks when all answered 2xx; after 3 retries the message moves to `bakery-events-dlq`,
whose consumer writes it into the Sandbox's dead letters. Locally, `wrangler dev` runs both queues:
the log shows each failed delivery (`delivery_failed`), Wrangler's own line moving the message to
the DLQ, and `dlq` when the Sandbox keeps it, with `while_off` and `counted`. Since 2026-11-10
(roadmap R67) a dead letter that arrives while the flag is `off`, or within two minutes of the
flag's last change, is kept with `made_while_off: true` on the dead-letter list and its redrive,
and writes no `dlq` point (`"counted":false`): at `off` the consumer retries everything, so those
messages died of the switch. The dead-letter queue's consumer asks `Control` for the flag and its
`changed_at` once a batch (`bakery_app::dead_letters`). The mark says when, not why: a real fault
while `off` is marked too. A marked letter redrives as any other. `scripts/smoke-breaker.sh`
proves both a marked and a counted one.

## The kitchen socket

`GET /v1/sandboxes/{id}/kitchen/ws` upgrades to a socket the `Kitchen` accepts on the Hibernation
API, with the socket's own id in its attachment. Any session of the sandbox, a guest's included,
reads the board and sends its three moves (since 2026-11-10, roadmap R72). Frames are JSON text,
protocol v1, each with `"v":1`:

| Direction        | Frame                                                                                           | When                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| server to client | `{t: "snapshot", board: {cards, bake_lists}, seq}`                                              | on open; `seq` counts the events that changed the board; `bake_lists` below                                                   |
| server to client | `{t: "evt", env, bake_list?}`                                                                   | each event that changed the board, the envelope whole, the note with it; and the Sandbox's `ClockAdvanced`; `bake_list` below |
| server to client | `{t: "presence", sockets}`                                                                      | a socket opened or closed                                                                                                     |
| server to client | `{t: "bye", reason: "wiped" or "limit"}`                                                        | before the server closes: 1001 at the wipe; 1008, 1009 or 1003 at a limit                                                     |
| client to server | `{t: "cmd", id: <ULID>, order: <ULID>, action: start_production, mark_ready or mark_picked_up}` | a move on the board from any session, sent to the Order's `/_command` with `id` as the idempotency key                        |
| server to client | `{t: "ack", id}` or `{t: "nack", id, rule}`                                                     | the answer; a repeated `id` gets the first answer, after a wake too; a guest's command is answered as any session's           |

Since PR 6 the snapshot's `board.bake_lists` is `[{date, version, lines: [{item, qty, start}],
reserved}]`, one entry per bakery day from business today on that the board has heard of, sorted
by date: the day's latest `BakeListChanged` (`version` 0 before one) and how many places it has
reserved. `BakeListChanged`, `SlotReserved` and `SlotReleased` arrive as `evt` frames once the
Kitchen applied them; a `SlotReleased` for a place the board never saw reserved changes nothing
and is not sent. Since 2026-11-06 each of those frames also carries `bake_list`, the day as the
board holds it after the event, in the snapshot's entry shape. The client sets the day from it
and does not adjust its own count: a place reserved before the client's snapshot and released
while it watches was never seen reserved by the client, so a count it kept itself would stay one
too high until the next snapshot. A set is idempotent: a repeated frame changes nothing, and
after a missed one the day's next frame puts the count right.

`{"t":"ping","v":1}` is answered `{"t":"pong","v":1}` by the platform's auto-response
(`State::set_websocket_auto_response` exists in workers-rs 0.8.7), which does not wake the object;
the storefront sends none and reconnects instead. Limits: 4 open sockets a sandbox (a fifth is
accepted, told `bye` `limit` and closed with 1008, so the console knows not to retry), frames of
at most 4 KB (1009), 10 frames a second a socket (1008), text frames only (1003), and since
2026-11-10 a command budget for the sandbox: 60 commands reach its Orders at once, then one a
second, from all its sockets together; past it the answer is `nack` `rate-limit`, nothing is stored,
and a repeated id or an order not on the board spends nothing. The budget is in memory and starts
full at a wake. The last 64 command ids, the board and the heartbeat are in SQLite, so all of it survives hibernation; the
per-socket frame counts are in memory and start again after a wake. `wrangler dev` hibernates an
idle Kitchen with sockets open after about 10 s of quiet, though not punctually (seen on
2026-11-10: in the first 12 s, the third, or none of five; the smoke's idle spells therefore grow
from 12 s to 90 s): the next frame or upgrade logs `kitchen_wake` with a new instance mark. `scripts/ws-probe.mjs` is the smoke test's client: Node 22's `WebSocket`, the
cookie from a jar file, one JSON line per frame.

## The trace socket

The flight recorder (2026-11-09; [ADR 0015](../../docs/adr/0015-a-lossy-flight-recorder-beside-the-event-path.md)).
Once a message's deliveries are decided, the consumer in `src/queue.rs` builds its hop: the
envelope's `id`, `type`, `stream`, `seq`, `cause`, `at` and `recorded_at`, `delivered_at`, `lag_ms`
and each routed class with its outcome (`delivered`, `retried` or `dead_lettered`), never `data`.
At the end of the batch it sends each sandbox's `Recorder` one `/_record` under `wait_until`, after
the acks; a failure logs `trace_drop` with the sandbox, the count and the status. The Recorder keeps
the last 200 batches. A hop's `attempt` is its own count of the hops it recorded for the event, a
lower bound on the Queue's attempts, since workers-rs 0.8.7 exposes no attempt count; a retry made
with the flag `off` is not counted. A dead letter is never inferred from it: once the dead-letter
queue's consumer has kept a message, it sends the Recorder the event (`/_dead`), and the dead hop
marks `dead_lettered` the routes from the one that had not answered on the event's last recorded
hop, or every routed class when the Recorder holds no earlier hop. `GET /v1/sandboxes/{id}/trace?after=<cursor>` reads it; the socket at
`GET /v1/sandboxes/{id}/trace/ws` watches it. Frames are JSON text, protocol v1, each with `"v":1`,
typed by the `TraceFrame` contract:

| Direction        | Frame                                       | When                                                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| server to client | `{t: "snapshot", hops: [TraceHop], cursor}` | on open: the hops of the last 50 batches, newest last; `cursor` as `GET .../trace` gives it |
| server to client | `{t: "hops", hops: [TraceHop], cursor}`     | each batch recorded after the snapshot                                                      |
| server to client | `{t: "bye", reason: "wiped" or "limit"}`    | before the server closes: 1001 at the wipe; 1008, 1009 or 1003 at a limit                   |

`{"t":"ping","v":1}` is answered `{"t":"pong","v":1}` by the platform's auto-response, as on the
kitchen socket; the socket is read only, and any other client frame is ignored. The checks and
limits are the kitchen socket's: the exact `Origin`, the cookie only, `UPGRADE_LIMIT`, 4 open sockets
a sandbox, 4 KB frames, 10 frames a second, text only. The socket has no replay: a client that
reconnects reads `GET .../trace?after=<cursor>` for what it missed.

## Simulated traffic

`simulator:<sandbox>` is born on the first "Start traffic" and wiped with the sandbox. Its alarm
ticks once a wall-time minute: it asks the Kitchen's `/_presence`, and with no socket and no
heartbeat for 90 s it stops with `console_closed`; otherwise it spends one of the minutes
`Control` granted and places up to `rate` orders as the seed's other personas, drawn from the
seed's ChaCha on the minute's own stream, through the Sandbox's `/_register-order` and the
Order's `/_place` with the key and `cause` `sim:<ulid>`, each after the same hold a visitor's order
takes; a hold the day refuses is logged `sim_slot_full` with its rule and skipped, never retried.
Since PR 6 a minute's orders spread over 16 quarter hours from about four hours out, each moved to
the next start the day takes. A seventh order in one wall-time minute,
across runs, is refused and logged `sim_rate`. `Control` keeps a `traffic (day, sandbox, minutes)`
table: `/reserve-traffic` grants what is left of `TRAFFIC_MINUTES_CAP` (30, a var) and
`/settle-traffic` gives back what a run did not use; a start with nothing left answers 503
`traffic-cap` and logs `sim_cap`. `SIMULATOR_MAX_RATE` is fixed at 6 in `objects/simulator.rs`.
The smoke script lowers the cap to 2 in its `.dev.vars`, the same code path as 30.

## Payments, with the fake Stripe

`scripts/fake-stripe.mjs` stands in for Stripe's API and its webhooks on 127.0.0.1:8791 (its
header lists what it models and where it differs from Stripe). With it running, these lines in

```sh
STRIPE_API_BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8791
STRIPE_SECRET_KEY=rk_test_not_a_key
STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET=whsec_not_a_secret
```

`STRIPE_API_VERSION`, when set, is sent as `Stripe-Version`; `STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, a
`pk_test_` var, is handed to the payment step when set. `Billing` (`billing:<sandbox>`, in

Stripe's test payment method `pm_card_visa`, one in six with `pm_card_chargeDeclined`. An expired

## The status counters, with the fake Analytics Engine

The `METRICS` binding (`analytics_engine_datasets`, dataset `bakery_events`) takes one point per
event: `sandbox_created` (`Control`'s admission), `order_placed` (the `Order`'s commit),
`payment_succeeded`, `payment_failed` and `refund_issued` (`Billing`'s commit), `wipe` (the
nightly wipe's last fan-out run in `Control`: sandboxes, objects, milliseconds) and `dlq` (the
dead-letter writer). Each holds the event's name in `blob1` and `index1` and numbers in its
doubles, nothing about a visitor, a sandbox or an IP. A write is fire and forget: a missing
binding logs `metrics_unbound` once per isolate and a refused write `metrics_error`, and neither
fails the request. On `wrangler dev` the runtime accepts a point and stores nothing a script can
read, so the smoke proves the read path instead.

The 15-minute cron runs the two kept queries (`counts_24h` and `last_wipe`, in `crates/app/src/analytics.rs`; 192 a day) against the SQL API
with `ANALYTICS_API_TOKEN` and `ANALYTICS_ACCOUNT_ID`, 5 s each, and stores the answer in
`Control` by command; `GET /v1/status` serves it. A missing secret logs `analytics_unconfigured`
with its name; a failed query logs `analytics_failed` with the query's name, a reason and the
status, and `Control` keeps the old snapshot as `stale`. `scripts/fake-analytics.mjs` stands in
for the SQL API on 127.0.0.1:8792; with it running, these lines in `.dev.vars` point the Worker at
it (the override is honored only for 127.0.0.1 and localhost, and `analytics_base_refused` is
logged for anything else):

```sh
ANALYTICS_API_BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8792
ANALYTICS_API_TOKEN=fake-analytics-token
ANALYTICS_ACCOUNT_ID=0000000000000000000000000000fake
```

Since 2026-11-10 the route also carries `sandboxes_past_wipe`, which is not an analytics number.
The 15-minute sweep in `Control` counts the sandboxes with no `wiped_at` more than 2 hours after
their nightly wipe, the first 10:00 UTC after each was made (`count_past_wipe` in
`crates/app/src/wipe.rs`), and stores the count with its time in `Control`'s `past_wipe` row. The
route reads that row and serves the count while it is at most 20 minutes old, and `null` before
the first sweep or once the cron has stopped. The 2 hours cover the wipe in progress: six fan-out
runs of 50 sandboxes (the day's cap of 300), each allowed 15 minutes of wall time, plus the sweeps
that retry a failure. A sandbox made after a day's wipe is due at the next one, so it is never
counted early. The sweep runs whether or not the analytics secrets are set, and its log line
carries the count (`"event":"sweep","rows":0,"past_wipe":0`). The morning check fails when the
count is above 0 (docs/operations.md, "The nightly wipe").

## The flag

Since 2026-11-10 (roadmap R85) every route reads `Control`'s flag, from the isolate's copy that
lasts 60 s (`src/flag.rs`; the rule is `bakery_app::flag_gate`). At `read-only` a command answers
503 `read-only`; reads, signing in, "Wipe now" and Stripe's webhook still work. At `off` every route
answers 503 `off` but `GET /v1/health`, `GET /v1/status` and the webhook. The board's socket answers
a command `nack` with the rule `read-only`, and at `off` ends at its next frame with `bye` `off` and
close 1013; the Simulator stops at its next tick with `read_only` or `off`. A change reaches an
isolate within about a minute. A refresh that fails keeps the last flag read and waits 5 s before
the next; only an isolate that has never read the flag falls back to `live`. Until that date only admission read the flag, so a `read-only` or
`off` bakery kept writing. `../../scripts/smoke-breaker.sh` proves it on `wrangler dev`, the 10:00
UTC wipe at `off` included.

## Notes

- `compatibility_date` is 2026-11-04. The date a config may carry is bounded by the workerd inside the Wrangler the lockfile resolves: 4.152.0's refused it (its newest was 2026-10-26) and 4.157.0's accepts it (docs/delivery.md, compatibility_date). The lockfile resolves 4.158.0 as of 2026-11-09.
- `Control`, `Account`, `Sandbox`, `Catalog`, `Order`, `Kitchen`, `Simulator`, `Billing`, `Schedule`, `CateringEvent`, `Customer`, `Crm` and `Inbox` are declared SQLite-backed in the `exports` block; CI fails if a `#[durable_object]` class is missing there.
- `SANDBOX_SEED`, a local `.dev.vars` line, gives every new sandbox that seed instead of a random one, so two sandboxes born from it can be compared; it is honored only when `API_ORIGIN` is a loopback host (`scripts/smoke-crm.sh` sets it).
- The Cache API works on `wrangler dev` 4.157.0 (checked on 2026-11-05: the menu's second read answers `Bakery-Cache: hit`). An entry is stored with `max-age=86400`, since no sandbox outlives the next 10:00 UTC; the response to the browser says `private, no-cache`.
- The `bakery-events` consumer's `max_batch_timeout` is 0, so a batch goes as soon as a message is there: the default wait (5 s on the platform, 1 s locally, as of 2026-11-05) would keep a board change from a second browser for longer than the second PR 4 allows.
- The rate-limit bindings work on `wrangler dev` 4.157.0 (checked on 2026-11-05: a second creation from one IP hash inside the minute gets 429). If a runtime lacks them, the Worker logs `rate_limit_unavailable` once per isolate and lets the request through; `Control`'s caps still bound the day.
- `API_ORIGIN` is a var rather than the request's own origin because `wrangler dev` rewrites the request's host to the route's, `api.bakery.ihaveasegway.com`, and the OAuth redirect URI must match the registered one exactly.

## The schedule, the clock and the loop guard

`Schedule` is one object per sandbox and bakery day (`objects/schedule.rs`). Its state is the
fold of its own events; each command writes them with their outbox rows, its key and its hold
timers in one transaction. Each held place has a `hold:<ref>` timer at its `expires_at` in
business time, converted to wall time with the sandbox's offset; the alarm decides
`Release {hold_expired}` for each one due, and `/_clock` moves every timer's wall time when the
clock moves. Every object arms its one alarm for its earliest timer; arming the wall time the
platform already holds writes nothing. The re-arm at the end of an alarm run is counted per
object per UTC day (`rearms`), once per distinct wall time, and the 101st is refused: the object
logs `{"event":"alarm_guard","reason":"rearm-cap","object":<class>}` and stops arming until the
wipe. `Control`'s flag is the whole demo's, with no switch for one sandbox, so the guard does not
flag anything read-only. The refusal is proven by `objects/stream.rs`'s unit tests;
`scripts/smoke-schedule.sh` proves the idempotence over HTTP.

## The catering saga

`CateringEvent` is one object per inquiry (`objects/catering.rs`), hosting the domain's
`Catering` aggregate. Every step is one transaction of that object: a route's command, a delivery
from the consumer, or a timer row. Its timers are `bakery_domain::catering::timers` of its state,
one row each (purpose `catering`, ids `reminder:follow_up`, `reminder:event_soon`,
`reminder:invoice_due`, `quote-expiry`, `deposit-lapse`), rewritten in the transaction of every
change and moved by `/_clock`; the alarm runs the due row's command under `timer:<row>:<at>`. A
cancellation, decline, expiry or closure leaves no row and logs `timers_cleared`.

The edges between objects: `accept` holds the day's place in the `Schedule` before the
acceptance is recorded; `PaymentSucceeded` (purpose `deposit`) is `RecordDeposit`, and
`CateringDepositPaid` reserves the day; `CateringDepositLapsed` and `CateringCancelled` release
it, `CateringClosed` fulfils it; `CateringDelivered` makes `Billing` create the persona's Stripe
customer once (`<sandbox>:<persona>:customer`) and the invoice under the delivery's envelope id
(draft, item, finalize), whose `InvoiceIssued` is `RecordInvoice`; `invoice.paid` is
`PaymentSucceeded` (purpose `balance`), then `RecordBalance`, `CateringBalancePaid` and
`CateringClosed`; `CateringCancelled { refund_deposit: true }` makes `Billing` refund the deposit,
found by purpose and ref. A deposit that lands after its quote expired is refunded in full by
`Billing` under the delivery's id (reason `goodwill`, logged `deposit_late`). Billing's `/_wipe`
voids open invoices and deletes the sandbox's Stripe customers before `delete_all`, best effort,
and logs `stripe_wipe` with the counts.

Every Stripe idempotency key begins with the sandbox id since PR 7,
`<sandbox>:<ref>:<purpose>[-call]:<n>`: two sandboxes born from one seed share their seeded refs,
and Stripe keeps a key for 24 hours ([idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests), as of 2026-11-05).

## CRM, campaigns and the inbox

Three objects since PR 8 (`objects/customer.rs`, `objects/crm.rs`, `objects/inbox.rs`), all
seeded or born through the Sandbox and wiped with it.

- `customer:<sandbox>:<persona>` hosts the domain's `Customer`: consent and loyalty, the fold of
  its own events. It is born on first touch, a consent change or an order's `OrderPaid`, from the
  persona's profile, consent and opening points, which the Sandbox keeps in a `personas` table
  written at creation and hands out at `/_register-customer`; its first command is `Register`.
  `OrderPaid` earns a point a whole dollar, `OrderRefunded` takes them back, never below zero.
- `crm:<sandbox>` keeps the domain's `CrmProjection` as one serialized row, an append-only `log`
  of every envelope that changed it (the note and the brief removed), and the campaigns, the fold
  of its own events. It is seeded with the seed's roster and its history's `OrderClosed`
  envelopes (722 for seed 42, `PINNED_HISTORY_LEN` in `crates/seed`, as of 2026-11-05), before
  the seeded orders, whose own events then reach it through the Queue; the seeded catering events' events, which no outbox relays, are read from their logs and
  handed to it as each is seeded. A campaign's `send:<campaign>` timer decides `SendCampaign`
  with `Audience::of` the projection at that moment, under `timer:send:<campaign>`; a second
  firing sends nothing. The rebuild keeps the projection as it stood (`shadow`), truncates the
  projection to the roster, and replays 200 log entries a run, 300 ms apart; a delivery meanwhile
  is decided against the shadow and appended to the log for the replay, sends wait for the alarm
  after it, and reads answer the partial projection with `Projection-State: rebuilding`. It ends
  with `ProjectionRebuildFinished {events, ms}` and logs `rebuild_finished` with `identical`,
  whether the replay gave the shadow back byte for byte. `GET /_projection` on the object answers
  the stored projection's text, the debug read; `scripts/smoke-crm.sh` compares the stored row
  itself, since nothing outside the Worker reaches an object's paths.
- `inbox:<sandbox>` keeps one row a message and the domain's state but its messages (the order and
  catering facts, the ids seen) in one row. It is seeded with the seed's 240 receipts. Each new
  message's `MessageDelivered` goes to its own outbox in the transaction that records the
  consumed event, with an `open:<message>` and a `click:<message>` timer when the seed's
  engagement draws one; the alarm records each at its own business time and relays
  `MessageOpened` and `MessageClicked`. The `inbox` fault switch, copied here by the Sandbox,
  makes it refuse deliveries with 503, as the Kitchen's does.

The consumer delivers to all three: Crm and the Inbox are the sandbox's one, and a 410 from either
(not yet seeded, or wiped) counts as delivered; the Customer is the order's, which the Order's
`/_customer` names, registered with the Sandbox when it answers 410. Crm and the Inbox never get a
`note` or a `brief` on any event (`routing::strip_for`). Every message is an Inbox row whose
recipient is a customer id, `to: Ulid` on `MessageDelivered` (`crates/domain/src/inbox.rs`), so
none can hold an address (Q22). The `Mailbox` port that once stood for a sender had no
implementation and was removed on 2026-11-10 (roadmap R68).

## Dispatch

`objects/dispatch.rs` since PR 9 ([ADR 0012](../../docs/adr/0012-delivery-dispatch.md)):
`dispatch:<sandbox>:<YYYY-MM-DD>` hosts the domain's `Dispatch`, the fold of its own events. It
is born on first touch through the Sandbox's registry (`/_register-dispatch`, the same days as a
`Schedule`'s), and today's is seeded from `bakery_seed::seed_dispatch`: the open delivery orders'
stops, confirmed when paid, and the server's proposal (4 vans on a Saturday, 1 otherwise, 250
units a van), never published. Its only timers are the outbox's and the backstop.

- **The matrix** is `crates/seed/data/salt-lake-city/matrix.json`, compiled in
  (`bakery_seed::CITY_MATRIX_JSON`) and read by `TravelMatrix::from_json` once per isolate; it
  rides in the commands that plan and is never stored. A persona's place is the seed's `p<n>`,
  index `n − 1` whatever the fixture's labels say (`TravelMatrix::place_index`); the label the
  response shows is the fixture's map name ("The bakery" for index 0, `p1`).
- **The consumer** delivers `OrderPlaced` for a delivery (`AddStop`, the customer's place from the
  Sandbox's `/_place/<customer>`), `OrderPaid` (`ConfirmStop`), `OrderExpired` and
  `OrderCancelled` (`RemoveStop`) to the day of the order's window, reading the slot and kind
  from the Order for all but the first; a pickup's are acked unsent. `RoutePublished` goes to
  each stop's Order, which decides `MarkOutForDelivery {route: van-<n>, stop}` (an order not yet
  ready refuses, logged and acked), and to the Kitchen, which labels each card with its `van` and
  `stop`; `StopCompleted` goes to its Order, `MarkDelivered`. Since 2026-11-08 `OrderReady`
  reaches the day too (`ReadyStop`): an order made after its stop was published gets
  `StopReady {route, stop}`, once, and its Order decides `MarkOutForDelivery`, so it goes out
  when it is ready rather than when its stop is completed. `OrderOutForDelivery` reaches the day
  as well (`DepartStop`), and the stop reads `out_for_delivery` (`StopDeparted`) until it is
  completed.
- **A plan stands once a van has left.** Before a publish over a published plan the object asks
  that plan's Orders for their status; if one is out for delivery, delivered or closed, or the
  day has sent a stop out with `StopReady`, the aggregate refuses `published` (409). Completing
  stops still works.
- **The figures are the object's.** The browser's plan carries stop lists only; its minutes, the
  vans' units and the time the last van is back (09:00 plus the longest drive and 3 minutes at
  each door, the route data's `serviceSeconds`) come from the matrix.
